# Publication figure captions and insertion guide

These figures were generated by `scripts/generate_publication_figures.py`. Figure D reads the verified architecture and SPICE result CSVs directly; the script rejects any mismatch with the committed headline values. Figures A-C contain only architecture and methodology details already documented in the verified manuscript tables and evidence records. No physical dimensions, floorplan, TSV count, bandwidth, thermal field, sensing accuracy, or additional simulation output is introduced.

## Figure A - Proposed three-tier near-sensor architecture

**IEEE-style caption:** Fig. A. Proposed three-tier near-sensor organization comprising a conceptual sensing/front-end tier, a localized-memory tier, and a compute tier containing the evaluated 168-PE accelerator. Vertical arrows indicate conceptual inter-tier communication and do not specify physical dimensions, interconnect count, floorplan, or thermal boundaries. The evaluated architecture-level proxy models the localized tier as a 2-MiB SRAM; sensing accuracy and thermal behavior are outside the simulation boundary.

**Recommended insertion and supported claim:** Insert in Section III-A immediately after the first paragraph that introduces the three tiers. It supports the architectural contribution - heterogeneous sensing, localized storage, and compute are organized as separate logical tiers - while making the conceptual/simulated boundary explicit. It should replace or serve as the publication-quality version of the existing architecture schematic.

Files: `publication_figures/figure_A_three_tier_architecture.pdf`, `.svg`, and `.png`.

## Figure B - Controlled planar-versus-localized experimental comparison

**IEEE-style caption:** Fig. B. Controlled planar and localized-memory configurations. Both cases retain the same 14 x 12 (168-PE) compute array, per-PE input/weight/partial-sum register files, 128-KiB global SRAM, arithmetic precision, fixed mapping, and 1-ns clock. The controlled architectural change is the highest-level memory placement and type: external LPDDR4 in the planar baseline versus the evaluated 2-MiB localized SRAM. Highest-level event counts, Timeloop cycles, and utilization remain unchanged.

**Recommended insertion and supported claim:** Insert in Section III-C after the paragraph defining the planar baseline and controlled variant, or in Section IV-G beside the fair-comparison protocol. It supports the claim that the energy comparison isolates the highest-level memory substitution rather than changing compute throughput, mapping, event count, cycles, or utilization.

Files: `publication_figures/figure_B_controlled_planar_vs_localized.pdf`, `.svg`, and `.png`.

## Figure C - Two-level evaluation methodology

**IEEE-style caption:** Fig. C. Independent two-level evaluation methodology. The architecture path applies fixed Timeloop mappings to AlexNet CONV1 and the AlexNet-derived dense CONV2 configuration, obtains action counts, cycles, and utilization, and uses Accelergy with CACTI-backed component characterization to estimate architecture-level energy. The circuit path uses the exact `45nm_HP.pm` model with ngspice 42 to evaluate a representative CMOS driver, capacitive link, and receiver for switching energy and propagation delay. The paths meet only at bounded cross-level interpretation; they are not co-simulated and their percentages are not combined.

**Recommended insertion and supported claim:** Insert in Section IV-A immediately after the paragraph introducing the two independent evaluation paths. It supports the reproducibility and scope argument by showing what each tool produces and by preventing the reader from interpreting the architecture and circuit studies as a coupled simulation.

Files: `publication_figures/figure_C_two_level_methodology.pdf`, `.svg`, and `.png`.

## Figure D - Verified evidence summary

**IEEE-style caption:** Fig. D. Verified architecture- and circuit-level evidence. Timeloop/Accelergy total energy decreases from 7,343.23 to 2,256.65 µJ for AlexNet CONV1 (69.27%) and from 3,103.78 to 2,261.55 µJ for the AlexNet-derived dense CONV2 configuration (27.14%); paired Timeloop cycles and utilization are unchanged. In the separate ngspice 42 link testbench, the conservative 160-fF planar-link proxy produces 95.489 fJ/toggle and 27.367-ps mean propagation delay, whereas the 40-fF TSV reference produces 34.598 fJ/toggle and 14.675 ps, corresponding to 63.77% lower switching energy and 46.38% lower link delay. Link delay is not inference latency.

**Recommended insertion and supported claim:** Insert at the end of Section V-G, after the cross-level interpretation paragraph, or use as the manuscript graphical summary. It supports only the verified headline comparisons and the central conclusion that the architectural energy benefit is workload dependent while lower physical communication loading independently reduces representative-link energy and delay. It does not support an end-to-end speedup, reduced Timeloop cycles, improved utilization, or a combined architecture-plus-link percentage.

Files: `publication_figures/figure_D_verified_evidence_summary.pdf`, `.svg`, and `.png`.
