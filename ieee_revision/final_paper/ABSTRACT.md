# Abstract

Data movement can dominate edge artificial-intelligence accelerator energy when activations and model parameters repeatedly traverse an external-memory interface. This paper presents a three-tier near-sensor architecture that vertically organizes sensing, localized memory, and compute. Timeloop and Accelergy, with CACTI-backed memory characterization, evaluate the architectural mechanism. Two workloads—AlexNet CONV1 and an AlexNet-derived dense CONV2 configuration—execute on the same 168-processing-element, 45-nm accelerator with identical precision, mappings, lower memory hierarchy, and 1-GHz clock assumption. Replacing modeled external LPDDR4 with a 2-MiB localized SRAM reduces total energy from 7,343.23 to 2,256.65 µJ (69.27%) for CONV1 and from 3,103.78 to 2,261.55 µJ (27.14%) for dense CONV2. Timeloop cycles and utilization remain unchanged, establishing an energy-locality benefit rather than an inference-latency improvement. A 2–16-MiB CACTI-backed sweep shows that the CONV1 reduction decreases from 69.27% to 28.95% as SRAM capacity and access cost increase. Complementary ngspice 42 simulation with the exact public 45-nm high-performance PTM card compares a 40-fF TSV reference and a conservative 160-fF planar-link proxy derived from the reported capacitance relationship. Switching energy decreases from 95.489 to 34.598 fJ/toggle (63.77%), and mean propagation delay decreases from 27.367 to 14.675 ps (46.38%). These circuit-level results are reported separately from the architecture-level energy totals and do not imply an equivalent inference-latency improvement. Together, the two evaluations show that the benefit of 3D locality depends on both workload-level memory behavior and physical communication loading.
